Nothing you type here is saved or sent anywhere. The whole model runs inside this page, on your own device. There is no account, no server and no tracking, and the page makes no requests to the internet at all — so no one else can see your figures, us included. Close the tab and everything is gone. To keep a copy, use Export PDF before you leave.

What this is for. A forecast, not a budget. It answers one question: can you afford the big things you want — especially travel — while keeping enough money within reach? Fill in what you earn and spend, list the trips you are weighing up, and it shows what you would have left each year and where the tight moments fall.

Plain-English guide to the words in here
Liquid
Money you could actually get hold of — savings accounts and the ISA. It is the figure that decides whether a plan is comfortable or not.
Pension
Also yours, but locked away until much later, so this model never spends it. It is tracked separately for that reason.
Fixed savings
Money tied up for a set term. Better interest, harder to reach in a hurry.
Flex savings
An ordinary instant-access account. This is what gets spent first when a year costs more than it earns.
ISA
A UK wrapper for savings or investments where growth is tax free, up to a yearly limit. Anything above the limit goes to flex savings instead.
Salary sacrifice
Pay you give up before it is taxed, sent to your pension. It cuts your take-home but cuts your tax and National Insurance too.
Surplus
What is left in a year after tax, rent, living and travel. Negative means that year eats into savings.
Safety floor
A line you pick yourself: the least liquid money you are willing to be left with. The model flags years that fall below it rather than judging them.
Base-year money
Today’s prices. Trips are entered this way and then inflated to the year you actually travel, so you budget against the price you will really be quoted.
Stress case
The same plan re-run with harsher assumptions: travel and living rising faster, returns lower, bonus and extra income cut to three quarters. If it survives that, it is robust.
Savings rate
The share of your net income you keep. A rough health check — higher is more resilient.

How it works. Salary goes in year by year and is taxed; bonus and extra income are entered as they land in your account. Rent, living, the recurring trip and ad hoc spending inflate forward automatically, and you can type over any year.

    Where do these rates come from?

    Living at 3% is the Bank of England’s 2% CPI target plus a margin, and sits close to the UK’s long-run average. Pay rises at 1.5% is deliberately cautious: below the living rate, so your salary slowly loses purchasing power unless you raise it. Set it to match living inflation to hold even. Travel at 5% is deliberately higher: flights, cruises, safaris and expedition trips have historically risen faster than general prices, and premium travel faster still.

    These are starting points, not forecasts, and nobody knows the real figures. Change any of them and the whole model follows — and the stress case shows what harsher assumptions would do.

    Tax detail — check and correct the bands

    Bonus and extra income are entered after tax. Bonus, fixed income and side-project income are all taken as net cash received — only salary runs through the tax, National Insurance, pension and student-loan calculation. Return on savings is treated as net of fees and tax. Rent now inflates at the general rate like living costs, with a per-year override if you know the actual figure.

    Tax figures are simplified single-person estimates, not payroll calculations. Only salary runs through them; bonus and extra income are entered net. The UK model is the most complete. Others omit regional and local taxes, household allowances and most reliefs — Germany and France in particular replace a continuous formula with bands, so expect a few per cent of error. Every band, allowance and rate is editable under Tax detail; correct them against a local payslip before relying on the output.

    Future travel costs are inflated at 5% annually by default. This is intentionally higher than the general living-cost assumption because premium hotels, flights, cruises, safaris and expedition travel can rise faster than ordinary consumer prices. All long-term projections are estimates rather than guarantees.

    Pension sacrifice is taken off salary before tax, social contributions and any extra deduction. All thresholds are held flat across every year, so real-world indexation is not modelled. Pension contributions are not counted as savings, even though they are yours. The model cannot capture sacrificing part of a bonus, because the bonus is entered net. Not tax advice.
